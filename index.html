The Problem

Most stalled deals aren't a sales problem. They're a capital problem.

A homeowner wants the project. They just don't have $30–90K in cash sitting around — and most of them are sitting on far more than that in home equity they've never touched.

Cash offers lose deals

Asking a homeowner to write a check on the spot is the single biggest reason quotes go cold. Financing keeps the conversation moving instead of ending it.

Equity is the underused asset

Home equity lending unlocks capital homeowners already have, at rates well below unsecured personal loans or credit cards — without touching their first mortgage.

Common Questions

What contractors and homeowners ask us most.

Straight answers on how home equity financing works through this partnership.

What's the difference between a HELOC and a home equity loan?+
A home equity loan gives you a single lump sum with a fixed repayment schedule. A traditional HELOC (home equity line of credit) instead lets you draw funds as needed over time, up to an approved limit. Figure's HELOC is a bit of a hybrid: it requires the full approved amount to be disbursed at closing, similar to a home equity loan, but keeps the line open so homeowners can redraw funds as they repay during the draw period.
How fast can financing be approved for a project like solar or a roof replacement?+
Homeowners typically complete the online application in about five minutes, and many receive a credit decision the same day — a meaningfully faster timeline than a traditional home equity loan, which can take several weeks. This makes it practical to offer financing at the point of sale rather than asking a homeowner to wait before signing a contract.
Does checking eligibility affect a homeowner's credit score?+
The initial eligibility check uses a soft credit pull, which does not affect the homeowner's credit score. A hard credit inquiry only occurs later in underwriting, once the homeowner chooses to move forward with a full application.
Will taking a HELOC affect a homeowner's existing mortgage?+
No — a HELOC or home equity loan sits behind the homeowner's existing first mortgage rather than replacing it. Homeowners keep their current mortgage rate and terms untouched while accessing equity separately.
How does the contractor get paid once a homeowner is approved?+
Figure disburses the full approved loan amount directly to the homeowner's account at closing, typically within about 5 business days — there is no lender-managed milestone draw process to the contractor. The homeowner then pays your business according to whatever payment schedule is set out in your own contract with them.
Which trades and project types qualify for this financing?+
The financing structure applies broadly to home-improvement projects a homeowner is investing in their property for, including solar and battery storage, roofing, windows and doors, HVAC replacement, full remodels, and other qualifying home improvement work.
How much can a homeowner borrow?+
Loan amounts through Figure's home equity products range up to $750,000, depending on the homeowner's available equity, credit profile, and underwriting approval.
Who actually originates and underwrites the loan?+
Home Equity Lending LLC is a marketing affiliate and does not originate, underwrite, or fund loans. All financing is originated by Figure Lending LLC, an equal opportunity lender. Approval, rates, and terms are determined by Figure's underwriting guidelines and vary by state.
