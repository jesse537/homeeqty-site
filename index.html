Why Homeowners Look

The most common reasons people tap their equity.

Every one of these uses your home as collateral. That is the tradeoff to weigh first.

Home improvements

Repairs and upgrades

A new roof, solar, windows, HVAC or a remodel: projects that add to the home that secures the line.

Debt consolidation

Paying off credit cards

Some homeowners combine card balances into one line. It only helps if new balances stop growing.

Your home secures the line. Using it to pay off credit cards turns unsecured debt into debt secured by your home, and if you cannot repay, you could lose your home.

Business owners

Funding a business

Lenders set their own rules on business use, so ask before you apply. If the business cannot carry the payments, your home is at risk.

Big expenses

Large planned costs

Tuition or another major cost you can plan for. Borrow only what you need and know how you will repay it.

A safety net

Access you may not use

Some people open a line and leave it alone. Ask about any costs to keep it open before you do.

Not sure yet

Just exploring

Knowing your equity is useful even if you never borrow. Start with the basic math above.

Your Options

How the main ways to borrow compare.

General education, not advice. Each lender sets its own terms, so ask for the full terms in writing before you sign anything.

Option How you get the money Your current mortgage What backs it
HELOC A line of credit you draw from as needed, up to a limit Stays as it is Your home
Home equity loan One lump sum, repaid on a set schedule Stays as it is Your home
Cash out refinance One lump sum at closing Paid off and replaced by a new, larger mortgage Your home
Personal loan One lump sum, repaid on a set schedule Not affected Usually nothing; based on your credit

Sources for further reading: the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau on home equity borrowing, and the IRS Publication 936 on home mortgage interest.

Risks In Plain Language

Read this before you borrow against your home.

  • A HELOC is secured by your home. If you cannot repay it, you could lose your home.
  • Using a HELOC to pay off credit cards turns unsecured debt into debt secured by your home.
  • Payments on a HELOC can change over time, and they can rise when the repayment period starts.
  • A cash out refinance replaces your whole mortgage, not only the new cash.
  • When you sell your home, any HELOC balance generally has to be paid off from the sale.
  • Borrowing more than you need, or for spending you cannot repay, puts your home at risk for no good reason.
Common Questions

What homeowners ask us most.

Last updated November 18, 2026

Is a HELOC a smart way to pay off credit cards?
A HELOC can pay off credit cards, but it turns unsecured credit card debt into debt secured by your home, and your home is the collateral, so if you cannot repay, you could lose your home. Before you decide, compare the HELOC's full repayment terms with what you owe on the cards now, and plan how you will keep new balances off the cards.
HELOC or cash out refinance: which is better for me?
A HELOC keeps your current mortgage in place and adds a separate line of credit you draw from as needed, while a cash out refinance replaces your mortgage with a bigger one and pays you the difference in cash. The choice depends on whether you want to keep your current mortgage and whether you need the money all at once or over time. Both are secured by your home.
How fast can I get a HELOC?
It depends on the lender and on how quickly you provide what they ask for. Figure's website explains its own steps.
Does checking my options affect my credit?
Ask the lender before you start. Lenders check credit in different ways and at different steps, and Figure's website explains how it does it.
Can I use a HELOC for my business?
Generally yes. Money drawn from a HELOC can be used for many purposes, including a small business, though each lender sets its own rules on how funds may be used. The line is secured by your home, so if the business cannot support the payments, your home is at risk.
What happens to a HELOC when I sell my home?
The HELOC is generally paid off in full from the sale, along with your first mortgage, before you receive what is left. Check your own loan agreement for the exact terms.
