Home Equity Readiness Checklist

What to know before you tap your home equity

Work through these five steps before you apply anywhere. You can print this page and tick each box.

Step 1. Know your numbers

  • Estimate your home value. Look at recent sales of similar homes nearby and your county assessor's record. Assessed value and market value are often different.
  • Find your mortgage balance on your latest statement. Add any other loan secured by your home.
  • Estimate your equity: home value minus everything you owe on the home. The Equity Check above does the math, and nothing you type is saved.

Step 2. Get clear on the purpose

  • Write down what the money is for and the total you need.
  • Decide whether you need it all at once or a little at a time. Ask each lender how its draws work, because products differ.
  • Make a repayment plan before you borrow, based on your regular budget.

Step 3. If you plan to pay off debt

  • Understand the trade. A HELOC is secured by your home. Using it to pay off credit cards or other unsecured debt turns that unsecured debt into secured debt. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • Have a plan to keep card balances from building back up.

Step 4. Compare your options

  • Compare a HELOC with a cash out refinance, a personal loan and a 401k loan. For each one, ask: Is my home the collateral? Does it replace my current mortgage? What are the full written terms and costs, and can they change over time? What happens if I sell the home or my plans change?

Step 5. Before you apply

  • Have your latest mortgage statement, homeowners insurance details and property tax bill handy. The lender will tell you what else it needs.
  • Ask a tax professional how your plans affect your taxes. IRS Publication 936 covers home mortgage interest.
  • Read every disclosure the lender gives you before you sign anything.

Risks In Plain Language

Read this before you borrow against your home.

  • A HELOC is secured by your home. If you cannot repay it, you could lose your home.
  • Using a HELOC to pay off credit cards or other unsecured debt turns that debt into debt secured by your home.
  • HELOC payments can change over time, and they can rise when the repayment period starts.
  • A cash out refinance replaces your whole mortgage, not only the new cash.
  • When you sell your home, any HELOC balance generally has to be paid off from the sale.
