What are the risks of a HELOC?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 23, 2026.

The biggest risk of a HELOC is that your home is the collateral. If you do not repay as agreed, the lender can foreclose, and you could lose your home.

Payments can also change with a variable rate and rise when the repayment period starts, the lender can freeze or lower your line, opening and keeping a line can cost money, and if home values fall you could owe more than your home is worth.

Would I put my house behind this?

Every dollar you draw is debt secured by your home, whatever it pays for. So the question is not only whether you can use a HELOC for something, but whether you would put your house behind that expense.

A new roof that protects the home is different from a vacation. If it is something you would regret owing on, ask whether another option, or waiting, makes more sense.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Am I comfortable with my home as collateral? If I could not repay, I could lose it.
  • If my income dropped next year, could I still make the payments?
  • Could my budget handle a higher payment if the rate went up or the repayment period started?
  • Do I have emergency savings separate from this line?
  • How exactly will I pay this back, and by when?
  • Do I plan to sell soon? You are generally required to pay off a HELOC in full when you sell.

Risks in plain language

  • Your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • Payments can change. HELOCs usually have a variable interest rate, so the payment can move even if you borrow nothing new.
  • Payments can jump at repayment. After the draw period ends, payments are often much higher, and some plans require a large balloon payment. If you cannot pay a balloon payment, you could lose your home.
  • The lender can freeze or reduce the line, for example if your home's value falls a lot, so the money may not be there when you planned to use it.
  • Costs to open and keep a line add to what you pay. Ask for a written list before you sign.
  • If home values fall, you could owe more than your home is worth, which can make it harder to sell or refinance.
  • If you use a HELOC to pay off credit cards or other unsecured debt, that debt becomes secured by your home.
  • Drawing is easy, so it can be easy to keep borrowing for things you did not plan.
  • When you sell, you generally have to pay off the HELOC in full, so you keep less from the sale.

Common questions

Can I lose my house with a HELOC?
Yes. A HELOC is secured by your home. If you do not repay as agreed, the lender can foreclose, and you could lose your home.
Can the lender freeze or lower my HELOC?
Under many agreements, yes, in certain situations, such as a big drop in your home's value or a change for the worse in your finances. Read the agreement and ask how it handles this before you count on the money.
What happens when the draw period ends?
You can no longer draw from the line, and you repay what you owe during the repayment period. Payments are often much higher then, and some plans require a large balloon payment. Ask the lender what your payment would look like.
Can my HELOC payment go up?
Yes. HELOCs usually have a variable interest rate, so your payment can rise even if you borrow nothing new. Ask how the rate can change and whether there is a limit on how high it can go.
What does a HELOC cost besides interest?
It depends on the lender. Some charge costs to open a line, such as an appraisal or closing costs, and some charge fees to keep the line open or to close it early. Ask for a written list of every cost.
What if my home loses value?
Your equity shrinks, and you could owe more than the home is worth, which can make it harder to sell or refinance. A big drop in value can also lead the lender to freeze or lower your line.
Can I cancel a HELOC after I sign?
On your main home, federal law generally gives you three business days to cancel a home equity line or loan in writing, counted from when you sign and receive the required disclosures and notices.
