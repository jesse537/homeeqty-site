How much equity do I have in my Cape Cod home?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

Your equity is what your home is worth today minus everything you still owe on loans it secures. For Barnstable County, the Census Bureau puts the median value of owner occupied homes at $629,000 for 2020 to 2024, but your own number depends on your house, your town and what you owe.

Home values change over time and can go down as well as up, so a value from a few years ago may not match what a lender finds today.

How to work out your own number

  1. Start with a value. Recent sales of similar homes nearby and your town's assessed value are starting points, but an appraisal or the lender's own valuation may come in different.
  2. Add up everything the home secures: your mortgage balance, any home equity line you already have, and any recorded betterment such as a Cape Cod AquiFund septic loan.
  3. Subtract the total from the value. What is left is your equity, and a lender will only let you borrow against part of it.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Is your value based on a recent sale nearby, or on what homes sold for a few years ago?
  • Do you know every balance the home secures, including any septic or sewer betterment?
  • How would a drop in Cape prices change the cushion you want to keep?

Risks in plain language

  • If you borrow against your equity, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • If home values fall after you borrow, you could owe more than you expected relative to what the home is worth.
  • A home equity line can have payments that change over time.
  • Borrowing against equity leaves less for you when you sell.

Common questions

Can I borrow all of my equity?+
Usually not. Each lender decides how much of your home's value it will lend against, and it counts what you already owe on the home first.
Does a Cape Cod AquiFund septic loan count against my equity?+
Yes. Barnstable County records a Notice of Betterment Assessment at the Registry of Deeds for the amount borrowed, and the unpaid balance becomes due when you sell, so count it with what you owe.
Is my town's assessed value the same as my market value?+
Not always. The assessment is a starting point. A lender usually confirms value with its own appraisal or valuation.
Do summer houses on the Cape have equity I can use?+
They can, but some lenders do not lend on second homes and rules differ. See our answer on HELOCs for a Cape Cod second home.
