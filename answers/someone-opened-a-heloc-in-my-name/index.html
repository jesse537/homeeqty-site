Someone opened a HELOC in my name: what do I do?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

Call the lender's fraud department right away and ask it to close the account, then report the identity theft to the FTC at IdentityTheft.gov, which gives you a recovery plan and a report you can use with the lender and the credit bureaus.

Because a HELOC is secured by a home, also check your county's property records for a lien recorded against your home, and place a fraud alert or credit freeze so it is harder for anyone to open more credit in your name.

What to gather first

  • Whether the account could be one a co owner opened, or one left over from an old loan, rather than fraud.
  • The letter, bill or credit report entry that showed the account, with the lender's name and account number.
  • When you first noticed it, and whether statements have gone to an address you do not know.
  • A log for every call: the date, who you spoke with and what they said.

Risks in plain language

  • A HELOC uses a home as collateral. A line opened in your name against your home can be recorded as a lien, and it can stay on the record until the lender releases it.
  • A lien on the record can delay or block a sale or refinance until it is cleared.
  • Balances on a fraudulent account can show up on your credit reports until you dispute them.
  • The faster you call the lender, the less time there is for new draws on the line.
  • If the lender will not close the account or release the lien, a lawyer can explain your options.

Common questions

Will I have to pay back a HELOC I did not open?
Report it and dispute it rather than paying it. Tell the lender in writing that you did not open the account, and use your IdentityTheft.gov report to dispute it with the lender and the credit bureaus. If you get stuck, a lawyer can tell you your rights.
Should I place a fraud alert or a credit freeze?
You can do both. A fraud alert asks lenders to check it is really you, and you place it with one bureau, which tells the other two. A freeze keeps most lenders from checking your credit until you lift it, and you place it with each of the three bureaus. Both are free.
How do I check for a lien on my home?
Ask the county office that records property documents, often called the recorder, register of deeds or county clerk, to search the records for your property. Many counties let you search online.
Should I keep paying my real mortgage?
Yes. Keep paying your real mortgage and other bills as usual while you deal with the fraudulent account, so the fraud does not turn into a missed payment of your own.
How can I watch for this in the future?
Check your credit reports often, since you can get them free every week at AnnualCreditReport.com. Some county recorders also offer an alert when a document is recorded in your name, so ask yours whether it does.
