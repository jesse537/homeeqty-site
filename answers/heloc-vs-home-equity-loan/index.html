HELOC or home equity loan: what is the difference?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 17, 2026.

A HELOC is a line of credit you draw from as needed, usually with a variable rate, while a home equity loan pays you one lump sum, usually with a fixed rate and the same payment each month.

Both are loans secured by your home, so with either one, your home is at risk if you cannot repay.

HELOC and Home equity loan side by side
HELOCHome equity loan
How you get the moneyDraw as needed up to a limit during a set draw periodOne lump sum when the loan closes
Rate typeUsually variableUsually fixed
PaymentsCan change as the rate and your balance change, and can rise when repayment startsUsually the same each month
Interest is charged onOnly the amount you have drawnThe full loan amount from the start
Borrow againYes, during the draw period as you repayNo, you would need a new loan
CollateralYour homeYour home

Questions to ask yourself

  • Do you know the full amount you need now, or will costs come in stages?
  • Could your budget handle a payment that changes from month to month?
  • Some lenders let you lock a fixed rate on part of a HELOC balance. Ask whether that is offered and on what terms.

Risks in plain language

  • With either loan, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • HELOC payments can change and can jump when the repayment period starts.
  • Some plans end with a large balloon payment. Ask how the balance is repaid before you sign.
  • A HELOC lender may freeze or lower your line, for example if your home value drops.
  • Both can come with closing costs. Ask the lender for every cost to open and keep the loan.

Common questions

Which one has a fixed rate?
Home equity loans usually have a fixed rate. HELOCs usually have a variable rate, though some lenders let you fix the rate on part of the balance.
Do both use my home as collateral?
Yes. Both are secured by your home, usually as a second loan behind your mortgage. If you cannot repay either one, you could lose your home.
Is the interest tax deductible?
Under IRS rules, interest on a home equity loan or line is generally deductible only if you itemize and the money is used to buy, build or substantially improve the home that secures it. Ask a tax professional about your situation.
