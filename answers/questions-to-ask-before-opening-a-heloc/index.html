What should I ask before opening a HELOC?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 17, 2026.

Before you open a HELOC, ask how the rate can change, what it costs to open and keep the line, how the draw and repayment periods work, and whether the plan ends with a balloon payment.

The CFPB suggests getting estimates from three lenders and comparing the same terms side by side, and remember that your home is the collateral.

What to ask and Why it matters side by side
What to askWhy it matters
RateIs the rate variable, how often can it change, and is there a cap?Your payment can rise when the rate does
CostsWhat does it cost to open, keep and close the line, including any yearly or early closing fee?Costs add to what you pay beyond interest
Draw periodHow long can I draw, and is there a minimum draw or balance?Rules on draws can make you borrow more than you need

Freezing the lineWhen can you freeze or lower my line?You may not be able to draw when you need to

Questions to ask yourself

  • What exactly is the money for, and how much do you need?
  • Could your budget handle a higher payment if rates rise or when repayment starts?
  • How would you repay the balance if you had to sell or move sooner than planned?

Risks in plain language

  • With a HELOC, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • HELOC payments can change and can jump when the repayment period starts.
  • Some plans end with a large balloon payment.
  • A lender may freeze or lower your line, for example if your home value drops or your finances change.
  • Read every term before you sign, and compare the same terms across lenders.

Common questions

Can I cancel a HELOC after I sign?
Federal law generally gives you three business days to cancel a home equity line or loan on your main home in writing, counted from when you sign and receive the required disclosures and notices. The CFPB explains how it works.
What is a balloon payment?
It is a large payment of the remaining balance due at once, usually at the end of the plan. Ask whether a plan has one and how you would pay it.
Why compare more than one lender?
Terms, costs and rules differ by lender. The CFPB suggests getting estimates from three lenders and comparing the same items on each.
