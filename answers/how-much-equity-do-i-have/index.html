How much equity do I have in my home?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

Your home equity is what your home is worth today minus everything you still owe on loans secured by it, such as your mortgage and any home equity loan or line.

For example, a home worth $400,000 with $250,000 still owed has about $150,000 in equity, and a lender will use its own estimate of the value and your payoff amounts, so its number can differ from yours.

Your own estimate and A lender's number side by side
Your own estimateA lender's number
Home valueRecent sales of similar homes nearby, online estimates or a past appraisalThe lender's own valuation, which may be an appraisal or an automated estimate
What you oweThe balance on your latest mortgage statementThe payoff amount on each loan secured by the home, which can include interest owed through the payoff date
Other loans on the homeEasy to leave out a second loan or an existing lineA title check shows the loans and liens recorded against the home
How exact it isA rough guide that can be off in either directionThe number the lender uses when it decides what it can offer

Questions to ask yourself

  • Do you have your latest statement for every loan secured by your home, not just the first mortgage?
  • Is your value estimate based on recent sales of homes like yours, or on a single online number?
  • How much equity do you want to keep in the home as a cushion if prices fall or you need to sell?

Risks in plain language

  • If you borrow against your equity, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • Home values can fall, and the equity you count on today can shrink.
  • Online value estimates can be wrong. Plan with a careful number, not the highest one you find.
  • Every dollar you borrow against the home lowers your equity until you repay it.

Common questions

What is the difference between home value and home equity?
Home value is what the home could sell for. Home equity is the part of that value you own, after subtracting what you owe on loans secured by the home.
Is my mortgage balance the same as my payoff amount?
Not quite. The CFPB explains that a payoff amount usually includes interest owed through the day you pay off the loan, so it can be higher than the balance on your statement.
Can I borrow all of my equity?
Usually not. Many lenders limit how much of your home's value can be borrowed against, so the line you are offered is often smaller than your full equity. Each lender sets its own limit.
