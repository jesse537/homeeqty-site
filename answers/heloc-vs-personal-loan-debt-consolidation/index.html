HELOC or personal loan to consolidate debt?

Last updated November 17, 2026

A HELOC is secured by your home and lets you draw as needed, while a personal loan is usually unsecured and paid out in one amount.

The bigger difference is risk: if you cannot repay a HELOC, you could lose your home, which is not true of an unsecured personal loan.

HELOC and personal loan side by side
HELOCPersonal loan
What backs itYour homeUsually nothing
How you get the moneyDraw as needed during the draw periodOne lump sum
If you cannot payYou could lose your homeCollections and credit damage
Can the payment changeOften yesSet by the loan agreement; ask
Debt you pay off with itBecomes secured by your homeStays unsecured
When you sell your homeGenerally paid off in fullNo change

Which one may fit

  • A personal loan may fit when the amount is modest, you want a set payoff date, and you do not want your home involved.
  • A HELOC may fit when you have substantial equity, a larger balance to consolidate, and confidence you can repay even if payments rise.
  • Either one works only if the old balances stay paid off.

Risks in plain language

  • With a HELOC, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • Unsecured debt becomes secured debt. Credit cards and most personal loans are not tied to your house; once a HELOC pays them off, you owe that money against your home.
  • HELOC payments can change, can jump when the repayment period starts, and some plans require a balloon payment.
  • The lender can freeze or reduce a HELOC, for example if your home's value falls.

Common questions

Is a HELOC better than a personal loan for debt consolidation?+
Not automatically. Compare the full cost of each offer for the amount you need, then weigh the bigger difference: a HELOC puts your home on the line, and an unsecured personal loan does not.
Does consolidating debt with a HELOC make the debt secured?+
Yes. Credit cards and most personal loans are unsecured. When a HELOC pays them off, you owe the same money against your home.
What happens to a HELOC if I sell my home?+
You are generally required to pay off the HELOC in full when you sell, according to the CFPB.
