Is a HELOC a smart way to pay off credit cards?

Written by [AUTHOR NAME, ROLE]. Reviewed by [REVIEWER NAME]. Last updated November 17, 2026.

Written for homeowners in Illinois.

It can be, for a homeowner with steady income and a plan to stop adding new card balances, because the CFPB notes that credit card interest is typically higher than borrowing that uses your home as collateral. The tradeoff is serious: paying cards with a HELOC turns unsecured credit card debt into debt secured by your home, so if you cannot repay, you could lose your home.

General education, not advice. Each lender sets its own terms, so ask for the full terms in writing before you sign anything.

When a HELOC payoff can make sense

  • You have built equity over years in the home and plan to stay.
  • You can afford the payment even if it rises when the repayment period starts.
  • You will stop using the paid off cards, so the balances do not come back.
  • You compare more than one offer. The CFPB suggests getting three.

When it usually does not

  • Your income is uncertain, or you are near retirement without a repayment plan.
  • The card balances came from spending that has not changed.
  • You would need to draw on the line just to cover the payments.

Risks in plain language

  • Your home is the collateral. If you fall behind or cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • Unsecured debt becomes secured debt. Credit card debt is not tied to your house. Once a HELOC pays it off, the same amount is owed against your home.
  • Payments can change. Many HELOCs have a variable interest rate, so the payment can move even if you borrow nothing new. [NEEDS APPROVED CLAIM: how Figure's fixed and variable draws work, source Figure's home equity line page]
  • Payments can jump at repayment. After the draw period ends, payments are often much higher, and some plans require a large balloon payment.
  • The lender can freeze or reduce the line, for example if your home's value falls.
  • You are generally required to pay off the line in full when you sell the home.

Common questions

Is it risky to use home equity to pay off credit cards?+
Yes, in one important way. The debt moves from unsecured to secured by your home, so if you cannot repay the HELOC, you could lose your home.
Will a HELOC lower what I pay on my credit card debt?+
It depends on the offer and how you repay. Ask each lender for the full repayment schedule, including what happens when the draw period ends, and compare it with what you owe now.
What should I do with the cards after I pay them off?+
Decide before you borrow. The plan only works if new balances stay off the cards, because owing on both the cards and the HELOC leaves you worse off than before.
