HELOC or reverse mortgage after 62?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 17, 2026.

A HELOC needs payments on what you draw, while a reverse mortgage, which is only for homeowners 62 and older, needs no monthly loan payment while you live in the home and its balance grows over time.

Both use your home as collateral, and with a reverse mortgage you must still pay property taxes, homeowners insurance and upkeep, so falling behind on either one can put your home at risk.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Could your income cover a HELOC payment through retirement, including a higher payment later?
  • Do you want to leave the home to family, and how would they repay a reverse mortgage?
  • Does anyone live with you who would not be on the loan?

Risks in plain language

  • With either loan, your home is the collateral. If you cannot meet the loan terms, you could lose your home.
  • HELOC payments can change and can jump when the repayment period starts.
  • A reverse mortgage balance grows every month, so the equity left for you or your heirs shrinks.
  • With a reverse mortgage, not paying property taxes or insurance, or not keeping up the home, can put the home at risk.
  • Anyone living in the home who is not a borrower or eligible spouse may have to move out or repay the loan when you move out or die.
  • The FTC says reverse mortgage fees and costs can be higher than other ways to borrow.

Common questions

Do I need counseling before a reverse mortgage?+
For the most common reverse mortgages, called Home Equity Conversion Mortgages, the FTC says you must meet with a HUD approved housing counselor first.
What happens to my heirs with a reverse mortgage?+
The loan has to be repaid when the last borrower or eligible spouse leaves the home, usually by selling it. For the common federally insured type, the CFPB says neither you nor your heirs will owe more than the home is worth.
Is reverse mortgage interest deductible?+
The FTC says interest on a reverse mortgage is not deductible until you pay the loan back. Ask a tax professional about your situation.
