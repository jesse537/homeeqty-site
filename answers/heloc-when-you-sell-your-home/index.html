What happens to a HELOC when I sell my house?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 17, 2026.

When you sell your home, you are generally required to pay off your HELOC in full at the same time as the sale, typically out of the sale proceeds.

Your mortgage and the HELOC are both paid off before you receive what is left, so what you take home is the sale price minus both balances and your selling costs.

What to expect from a HELOC when you sell
Your situationWhat to expect
The sale covers the mortgage, the HELOC and selling costsBoth loans are paid off and you keep the rest.
The sale covers the mortgage but not all of the HELOCYou would generally need other money to pay the rest before the sale can close.
You plan to draw on the HELOC to buy your next homeAsk your lender first, because the line generally must be paid off when the home that secures it is sold.
You are still in the draw periodEvery draw adds to what the sale must repay, so plan your draws around your sale date.

Before you list

  • Ask your lender for a current payoff amount.
  • Include the HELOC balance when you estimate what you will take home.
  • Tell your agent and closing company about the HELOC early, so the payoff is part of the closing.

Risks in plain language

  • The HELOC is secured by your home. Until it is paid off, if you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • If home prices fall, the sale may not cover both your mortgage and the HELOC.
  • If you used the HELOC to pay off credit cards or other unsecured debt, that debt became secured by your home and is now part of what the sale must repay.

Common questions

Do I have to pay off my HELOC when I sell my house?
Generally yes. Most HELOCs require you to pay off the line in full when you sell the home.
Can I keep my HELOC after I sell?
Generally no, because the line is secured by the home you are selling. Ask your lender how it handles a sale.
What if my sale does not cover my mortgage and HELOC?
You would generally need to cover the shortfall from other money before the sale can close, so talk with your lender before you list.
