Can I use a HELOC to buy out my ex in a divorce?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

Possibly: if the home will be yours and a lender approves a line based on your own credit and income, a HELOC can pay your ex their share, but your current mortgage stays in place, often with both of your names still on it.

A divorce decree does not change who owes the lender, so many people compare a HELOC with a cash out refinance or with taking over the mortgage in their own name, and with selling the home.

Four ways to handle the home, side by side
What happens to the current mortgageWhat to know
HELOCStays as it is, with whoever is on it nowYou draw what you need for the buyout. Payments can change, and you repay two loans secured by your home.
Home equity loanStays as it is, with whoever is on it nowOne lump sum, usually with a fixed rate and the same payment each month. You repay two loans secured by your home.
Cash out refinancePaid off and replaced by a new, larger mortgageThe new loan can be in your name alone, which takes your ex off the mortgage. The whole balance gets a new rate and term.
Sell the homePaid off from the saleNeither of you keeps the home. What is left is divided as your settlement says.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Can you carry the mortgage, the HELOC and the home's other costs on your income alone?
  • Will your ex stay on the current mortgage, and what does your settlement say if a payment is missed?
  • Does your settlement set a deadline for the buyout, and how long will the lender's steps take?
  • What will a lender ask for before it approves a line, such as the final decree or a new deed?
  • Have you asked your divorce lawyer how the buyout and any new loan should be written into the settlement?

Risks in plain language

  • Your home is the collateral. You alone would be repaying loans secured by the home, and if you cannot, you could lose it.
  • If your ex stays on the original mortgage, the lender can still hold both of you responsible for the payments.
  • Payments can change. Many HELOCs have a variable interest rate, so the payment can move even if you borrow nothing new.
  • Payments can jump at repayment. After the draw period ends, payments are often much higher, and some plans require a large balloon payment.
  • The lender can freeze or reduce the line, for example if your home's value falls.
  • Borrowing for the buyout lowers your equity, and you are generally required to pay off the line in full when you sell the home.

Common questions

Does the divorce decree take my ex off the mortgage?
No. The CFPB says divorce does not automatically change your relationship with creditors, and taking a name off the title does not take it off the mortgage. The person leaving is generally released only by the lender, by a refinance or when the other person takes over the loan with the lender's approval.
Can I take over the mortgage instead of refinancing?
Sometimes. The CFPB says a homeowner who wants the original borrower released generally has to take over the mortgage after meeting the lender's or investor's standards. Ask your mortgage servicer what it needs.
Why would someone use a HELOC instead of a cash out refinance?
A HELOC leaves your current mortgage and its rate in place and adds a second loan. A cash out refinance replaces the whole mortgage. Which one makes sense depends on your current rate, whether your ex needs to come off the loan and the costs of each, so ask lenders for written terms on both.
Is HELOC interest deductible if I use it for a buyout?
IRS Publication 936 says debt you take on to acquire a spouse's or former spouse's interest in a home because of a divorce or legal separation can be treated as home acquisition debt. Other limits still apply, so ask a tax professional how it applies to you.
Can I cancel a HELOC after I sign?
On your main home, federal law generally gives you three business days to cancel a home equity line or loan in writing, counted from when you sign and receive the required disclosures and notices.
