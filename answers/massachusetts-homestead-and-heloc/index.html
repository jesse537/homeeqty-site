Does a Massachusetts homestead protect my home if I take a HELOC?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

No. Massachusetts law makes a homestead subordinate to a mortgage signed by all owners of the home, so if your home equity line is secured by a mortgage you all signed, the homestead does not stop that lender from foreclosing if you do not repay.

Taking the line does not end your homestead. State law says a mortgage can only subordinate it, so it still protects up to $1,000,000 if you recorded a declaration, or $125,000 automatically, against many other creditors.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Have you recorded a declaration of homestead, or are you relying on the automatic amount?
  • Will every owner sign the mortgage for the line? If only some do, it takes priority only over the homestead of those who signed.
  • Is this home your principal residence?

Risks in plain language

  • Your home is the collateral for a home equity line. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home, and a homestead will not stop that.
  • Turning unsecured debt into a home equity line moves it from a debt your homestead may help with to one it does not.
  • Home equity line payments can change over time.
  • Homestead rules are detailed. Talk to a Massachusetts attorney about your situation.

Common questions

Will the lender make me release my homestead?+
No. Massachusetts law says a mortgage lender shall not require or record a release of homestead in connection with a mortgage.
Do I need to record a new homestead after I take a HELOC?+
The Norfolk County Registry of Deeds says no. Under the law, a mortgage cannot end a homestead, only subordinate it.
Does my spouse have to sign if they are not on the deed?+
State law says subordinating the homestead to a mortgage does not require the signature of a spouse who is not an owner. The lender may have its own requirements.
I recorded a homestead years ago. Do I get the higher amount?+
The declared amount rose from $500,000 to $1,000,000 in August 2024 under chapter 150 of the Acts of 2024. The Norfolk County Registry of Deeds says the increase applies to valid homesteads already on record, so you do not need to file again.
Will anyone tell me about homestead at closing?+
Yes. Massachusetts law says the closing attorney or settlement agent must give you notice of your right to declare homestead protection in every mortgage transaction.
