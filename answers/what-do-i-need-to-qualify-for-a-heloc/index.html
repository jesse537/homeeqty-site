What do I need to qualify for a HELOC?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

To qualify for a HELOC, lenders generally look at your home equity, your credit history, your income and how your monthly debt payments compare with that income.

Each lender sets its own standards and document list, so ask before you apply, and keep in mind that your home will be the collateral for the line.

What lenders look at and What you can check or gather side by side
What lenders look atWhat you can check or gather
Home equityYour home's value minus what you owe on itYour latest mortgage statement and a recent estimate of your home's value
Credit historyYour credit reports, including any late paymentsFree copies of your credit reports, so you can fix errors before you apply
IncomeWhether your income can support the paymentsRecent pay records or tax returns; IRS transcripts can help if you are self employed
Debt to incomeYour monthly debt payments compared with your monthly income before taxesA list of every monthly debt payment you make
The homeThe type of home and whether you live in itAsk whether the lender accepts your type of home

Questions to ask yourself

  • Have you checked your credit reports for errors recently?
  • Do your monthly debts leave room in your budget for a new payment that can change?
  • Do you have the income documents a lender is likely to ask for, such as recent pay records or tax returns?

Risks in plain language

  • With a HELOC, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • Qualifying for a line does not mean the payments fit your budget. Plan for the payment on the full amount you might draw.
  • Applying usually means a hard credit check, which can affect your credit scores.
  • HELOC payments can change and can jump when the repayment period starts.

Common questions

Do I need perfect credit for a HELOC?
No single credit standard applies to every lender. Each lender sets its own, so ask what it requires before you apply.
What is a debt to income ratio?
The CFPB defines it as all your monthly debt payments divided by your gross monthly income. Lenders use it to judge whether you can manage a new payment.
Does the home have to be my main home?
Lender rules vary. Some lend only on a main home, and others may accept a second home or a rental property. Ask before you apply.
Where can I get my credit reports?
The CFPB explains that you can get free copies of your reports from Equifax, Experian and TransUnion through AnnualCreditReport.com.
