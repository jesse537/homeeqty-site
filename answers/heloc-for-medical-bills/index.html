Is it smart to use a HELOC for medical bills?

Written by [AUTHOR NAME, ROLE]. Last updated November 17, 2026.

Using a HELOC to pay medical bills turns bills that are not secured by anything into debt secured by your home, so if you cannot repay, your home is at risk.

Before you borrow, the CFPB suggests checking the bill for errors, asking whether you qualify for the provider's financial assistance, and asking whether the provider will accept an interest free payment plan.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Have you asked for an itemized bill and checked that your insurance was applied correctly?
  • Have you asked the hospital or provider about financial assistance or charity care?
  • Will more bills come later, and how would you handle them if the line is already in use?

Risks in plain language

  • With a HELOC, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • If you use a HELOC to pay medical bills or other unsecured debt, that debt becomes secured by your home.
  • HELOC payments can change and can jump when the repayment period starts.
  • Once a bill is paid, it can be harder to dispute a charge or get financial assistance for it.
  • A health setback can also affect income, and HELOC payments do not pause while you recover.

Common questions

What should I do first if I cannot pay a medical bill?+
The CFPB suggests making sure you owe the amount, checking the bill for errors, asking about financial assistance and asking the provider about a payment plan.
Do nonprofit hospitals have to offer financial assistance?+
According to the CFPB, the Affordable Care Act requires hospitals with 501(c)(3) nonprofit status to have financial assistance programs, and some states have their own charity care rules. You can ask the hospital for its policy free of charge.
Does paying medical bills with a HELOC change what is at risk?+
Yes. An unpaid medical bill can hurt your credit and lead to collection. A HELOC is secured by your home, so if you cannot repay it, you could lose your home.
