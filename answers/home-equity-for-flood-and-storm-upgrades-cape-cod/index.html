Can I use home equity to protect my Cape Cod home from floods and storms?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

Yes, a home equity loan or line can pay for work like raising a home, adding flood vents, moving heating equipment above flood level or strengthening a roof, but you are borrowing against the house you are trying to protect. The state's coastal hazards handbook calls raising a home above the predicted flood level one of the most effective things a homeowner can do.

Get your town's permits and approvals before any work starts, as the handbook advises. If a flood has already damaged the home, flood insurance may pay for part of the work first.

Before you borrow

  1. Ask your town which permits and approvals the work needs, and get them before work starts.
  2. Get written estimates, and know which parts of the job you could do later if costs run high.
  3. If a flood has damaged the home, ask your flood insurer whether Increased Cost of Compliance coverage applies.
  4. Decide whether a one time loan or a line you draw from suits the project better. See our answer comparing a HELOC and a home equity loan.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Which upgrade does the most for your home's flood zone and wind exposure?
  • Will the work need town permits, and how long could they take?
  • If the project grows, would you borrow more or scale back?

Risks in plain language

  • If you borrow against your equity, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • A home equity line can have payments that change over time.
  • Borrowing against equity leaves less for you when you sell.
  • Construction costs can rise once work begins, so you could need more than you planned.

Common questions

What storm upgrades does Massachusetts suggest for coastal homes?+
The state handbook covers raising the home, flood vents and other flood retrofits, raising heating and other equipment, hurricane clips at the roof to wall connections, ring shank nails when you replace the roof, closed cell foam applied from the attic and bracing gable end walls.
Can flood insurance pay for raising my home?+
Sometimes. After a flood, if your town decides the home is substantially damaged, Increased Cost of Compliance coverage on a National Flood Insurance Program policy can pay up to $30,000 toward raising, moving or demolishing it to meet local rules.
Do I need permits for this kind of work?+
Usually. The state handbook says to get all required local, county and state permits and approvals before any work starts.
Should I use a HELOC or a home equity loan for the work?+
It depends on whether you need the money at once or in stages. See our answer comparing a HELOC and a home equity loan.
