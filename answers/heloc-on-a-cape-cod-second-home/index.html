Can I get a HELOC on my Cape Cod second home?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

Some lenders offer home equity lines on second homes and some do not, and those that do can set different rules than for a main home. It comes up often here: the state says about 36 percent of homes in Barnstable County are classified for seasonal, recreational or occasional use.

Two protections work differently on a second home. The federal right to cancel a home equity line within three business days covers only your principal dwelling, and Massachusetts homestead protection covers only your principal residence.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Does the lender treat your Cape house as a second home or as a rental property, and does that change what it offers?
  • Could you keep up with payments from your regular income if rental income is slow in the off season?
  • Would it make more sense to borrow against your main home or the Cape house, knowing either one becomes the collateral?

Risks in plain language

  • The home that secures the line is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose it.
  • Without the three day right to cancel on a second home, read every document before you sign, because you cannot undo it afterward under that rule.
  • A home equity line can have payments that change, and the balance must be repaid when you sell.
  • Rental income can drop or stop, but the line still has to be repaid.

Common questions

Do I get three days to cancel a HELOC on a second home?+
Not under the federal rule. The CFPB's official commentary says a consumer can only have one principal dwelling at a time and a vacation or other second home would not be a principal dwelling.
Does a declared homestead protect my Cape summer house?+
No. Massachusetts law defines a principal residence as the home where you live or intend to live as your primary dwelling, and you can hold homestead rights in only one.
Can I use the equity in my main home to fix up the Cape house?+
Some owners do. In that case your main home is the collateral, so you could lose your main home if you cannot repay.
