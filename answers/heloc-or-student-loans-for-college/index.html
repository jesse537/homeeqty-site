Should I use a HELOC for college or take out student loans?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 17, 2026.

Federal student loans come with federal options a HELOC does not have, such as deferment, forbearance and repayment plans set by law, and they do not put your home at risk.

A HELOC uses your home as collateral and stays your debt as the homeowner, so if you cannot keep up with payments, you could lose your home.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Have you filed the FAFSA and counted grants, scholarships and work study first?
  • Who is expected to repay, and would that change if plans change?
  • How close are you to retirement, and would a payment on your home continue into it?

Risks in plain language

  • With a HELOC, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • A HELOC stays your debt even if the student's plans change.
  • HELOC payments can change and can jump when the repayment period starts.
  • Drawing for several years of school adds up, and every draw is secured by your home.
  • Federal loan protections do not apply to a HELOC.

Common questions

Should I fill out the FAFSA even if I plan to use a HELOC?
The FAFSA is the form for federal grants, work study and federal student loans, and many schools use it for their own aid. Filing it shows you what is available before you borrow against your home.
Can I move a HELOC into my child's name later?
No. A HELOC is the homeowner's debt and stays secured by the home until it is repaid.
Is HELOC interest deductible if I use it for college?
Under IRS rules, home equity interest is generally deductible only when the money is used to buy, build or substantially improve the home that secures it. Ask a tax professional about your situation.
