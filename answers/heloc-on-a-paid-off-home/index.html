Can I get a HELOC on a home that is paid off?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

Yes. Owning your home free and clear does not stop you from applying for a HELOC, and the line would usually be the only loan secured by the home.

With no mortgage, more of the home's value counts as equity, which can raise what a lender is willing to offer, but the lender still weighs your credit history and income, and your home becomes the collateral for the line.

Questions to ask yourself

  • What exactly is the money for, and how much of a line do you actually need?
  • Could your budget handle the payment if your rate went up, your income dropped or repayment started?
  • Would you rather keep the home free of any loan, for example to leave it to family or keep a cushion in case you need to sell?
  • Is the home in your own name, or held in a trust or a company? That can change what the lender asks for.

Risks in plain language

  • With a HELOC, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • A paid off home has no loan against it today. Opening a line puts a lien on the home again until you repay the balance and close the line.
  • HELOC payments can change and can jump when the repayment period starts.
  • The lender may freeze or lower your line, for example if your home's value falls, so do not count on the full line always being there.
  • Every dollar you borrow against the home lowers your equity until you repay it.

Common questions

Will I get a bigger line because my home is paid off?
It can help. With no mortgage, more of the home's value counts as equity, which can raise what a lender is willing to offer. Each lender sets its own limit, and your credit history and income still matter.
Why does the lender check title on a home I own outright?
To confirm who owns the home and what loans or liens are recorded against it. A lien you did not know about, or a home held in a trust or a company, can slow things down.
How many homeowners own their home free and clear?
In 2025, 41.0% of U.S. owner occupied homes (35,883,325 of 87,511,734) had no mortgage or other home loan, according to the Census Bureau's American Community Survey. Our national home equity snapshot shows the share for every state.
Is HELOC interest tax deductible?
Under IRS rules, interest on a home equity loan or line is generally deductible only if you itemize and the money is used to buy, build or substantially improve the home that secures it.
Can I cancel a HELOC after I sign?
On your main home, federal law generally gives you three business days after signing to cancel a home equity line or loan in writing, counted from when you sign and receive the required disclosures and notices.
