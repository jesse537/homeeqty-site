Does checking HELOC offers hurt my credit?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

It depends on the kind of credit check: a soft inquiry does not affect your credit scores, while a hard inquiry, which usually happens when you formally apply, can lower them.

Before you share your information, ask each lender whether it runs a soft or a hard check to show you what you may qualify for.

Soft inquiry and Hard inquiry side by side
Soft inquiryHard inquiry
When it usually happensChecking your own credit, and some early looks at offersWhen you formally apply for credit

What to ask the lenderIs this first check a soft inquiry?At what step will you run a hard inquiry?

Questions to ask yourself

  • Are you ready to apply, or are you still comparing what lenders may offer?
  • Do you know which kind of check each lender runs before you share your information?
  • Have you looked at your own credit reports for errors first?

Risks in plain language

  • Checking offers is not borrowing, but if you open a HELOC, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • An early look at offers is not an approval. Terms can change after the lender's full review.
  • Several hard inquiries can add up on your credit reports.
  • Opening a new line and carrying a balance on it can also affect your credit.

Common questions

Does checking my own credit hurt my scores?
No. The CFPB explains that checking your own credit is a soft inquiry, and soft inquiries do not affect your credit scores.
Is a prequalified offer the same as approval?
No. It is an early estimate. The lender decides only after your full application and its review.
Can opening a HELOC affect my credit?
Yes. A new account, the balance you carry and whether you pay on time can all affect your credit.
