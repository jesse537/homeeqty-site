Can I use a HELOC to pay off my mortgage?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

Yes, if a lender approves a line large enough to cover the balance, you can use a HELOC to pay off your mortgage, but the debt does not go away: it moves from your mortgage to a line that is still secured by your home.

Most HELOCs have a variable rate and payments that can rise when the repayment period starts, while many mortgages have a fixed rate and a set payoff schedule, so the switch can make your payments harder to predict.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Is your current mortgage rate fixed, and would you give that up for a rate that can change?
  • Would the line cover your whole balance, or would you end up repaying both a mortgage and a HELOC?
  • Could your budget handle the payment if your rate went up or the repayment period started?
  • Does your mortgage have a prepayment penalty? Your servicer can tell you.
  • Would extra payments on the mortgage itself get you where you want to go without opening a new line?

Risks in plain language

  • Your home is the collateral for the HELOC, just as it is for your mortgage. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • Moving the balance does not lower what you owe. It moves the same debt to a different loan secured by the same home.
  • Payments can change. Many HELOCs have a variable interest rate, so the payment can move even if you borrow nothing new.
  • Payments can jump at repayment. After the draw period ends, payments are often much higher, and some plans require a large balloon payment.
  • The lender can freeze or reduce the line, for example if your home's value falls.
  • An open line is easy to draw on again, so you could end up owing more against your home than you started with.

Common questions

What is velocity banking?
It is a name used on social media for drawing on a HELOC to make large payments on a mortgage, then paying the line down with your paycheck. It does not lower what you owe by itself. The balance moves to a line secured by the same home, and whether you come out ahead depends on the rates, the costs of opening and keeping the line, and how fast you repay.
Would the HELOC become my first mortgage?
While your mortgage is in place, a HELOC usually sits behind it. Once the mortgage is paid off and its lien is released, the HELOC would usually be the only loan secured by the home.
Can I use a HELOC to pay down part of my mortgage?
Generally, yes. You could draw on a line and make an extra payment on your mortgage. Ask your servicer how extra payments are applied, and remember that you would then be repaying two loans secured by your home.
Is HELOC interest tax deductible if I use it to pay off my mortgage?
Under IRS rules, interest on a home equity loan or line is generally deductible only if you itemize and the money is used to buy, build or substantially improve the home that secures it. IRS Publication 936 also says secured debt used to refinance a mortgage you took out to buy your home is treated the same way, up to the old mortgage balance. Ask a tax professional how this applies to you.
Can I cancel a HELOC after I sign?
On your main home, federal law generally gives you three business days after signing to cancel a home equity line or loan in writing, counted from when you sign and receive the required disclosures and notices.
