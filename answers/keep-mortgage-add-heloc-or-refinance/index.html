Should I keep my current mortgage and add a HELOC instead of refinancing?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 17, 2026.

Adding a HELOC leaves your current mortgage, its rate and its payoff date as they are and puts a second loan on your home, while a refinance pays off your mortgage and replaces it with a new one.

Which costs less depends on how your current mortgage compares with a new one, how much you need and for how long, and both options use your home as collateral.

Keep mortgage, add a HELOC and Refinance side by side

Questions to ask yourself

  • How does the rate on your current mortgage compare with what a new mortgage would cost today?
  • Do you need one amount now, or money over time?
  • How long do you plan to stay in the home, and would restarting your mortgage term matter to you?

Risks in plain language

  • With a HELOC, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • With two loans on your home, falling behind on either one can put your home at risk.
  • HELOC payments can change and can jump when the repayment period starts.
  • A refinance can restart your payoff clock and raise the total interest you pay over time.
  • A cash out refinance makes your mortgage balance bigger.
  • Both come with closing costs. Ask for every cost before you compare.

Common questions

Does adding a HELOC change my first mortgage?
No. Your mortgage keeps its rate, payment and term. The HELOC is a separate loan that is also secured by your home.
Can I refinance later if I already have a HELOC?
Often, but the HELOC usually has to be paid off or the HELOC lender has to agree to stay in second place behind the new mortgage. Ask both lenders how they handle it.
What is the difference between a cash out refinance and a regular refinance?
A regular refinance replaces your mortgage with a new one for about the same balance. A cash out refinance replaces it with a larger loan and pays you the difference in cash.
