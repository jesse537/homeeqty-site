HELOC or cash out refinance: which is better for me?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 17, 2026.

A HELOC keeps your current mortgage in place and adds a separate line of credit you draw from as needed, while a cash out refinance replaces your mortgage with a bigger one and pays you the difference in cash.

The choice depends on whether you want to keep your current mortgage and whether you need the money all at once or over time. Both are secured by your home.

Questions to settle first

  • Do you need all the money now, or in stages? A HELOC lets you draw over time, and a cash out refinance pays out once.
  • Would replacing your mortgage change terms you want to keep? Compare the full terms of a new mortgage with your current one.
  • How long will you stay in the home?
  • Can you handle two monthly payments instead of one?

Risks in plain language

  • Both options use your home as collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • A cash out refinance resets your whole mortgage, not only the new cash.
  • A HELOC payment can rise, especially when the repayment period starts, and some plans require a balloon payment.
  • If you use either one to pay off credit cards or other unsecured debt, that debt becomes secured by your home.

Common questions

Is a HELOC cheaper than a cash out refinance?
It depends on the offers. Costs differ by lender and by loan, so ask each lender for a written list of every cost and compare the totals for the amount you actually need.
Can I have a HELOC and a mortgage at the same time?
Yes. If you already have a mortgage, a HELOC is a second mortgage that you repay alongside your first one.
Can I cancel a HELOC or a refinance after I sign?
For your main home, federal law generally gives you three business days to cancel for any reason without penalty. There are exceptions, such as refinancing with your current lender without borrowing more.
