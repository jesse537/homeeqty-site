HELOC or a business line of credit?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 17, 2026.

A HELOC is personal credit backed by your home, while a business line of credit is made to your business and is backed by the business, its assets or your personal guarantee, depending on the lender.

If you use a HELOC for the business and the business cannot keep up with payments, your home is at risk.

HELOC and Business line of credit side by side
HELOCBusiness line of credit
Who borrowsYou, personallyYour business
CollateralYour homeBusiness assets, a personal guarantee, or both, depending on the lender
What the lender looks atYour personal credit, income and home equityBusiness revenue, time in business, and often your personal credit
Shows up onYour personal creditBusiness credit, and personal credit if you guarantee it, depending on the lender
If the business strugglesYou still owe the HELOC, and your home is collateralThe business owes it, and you may owe it under a personal guarantee

Questions to ask yourself

  • How long has the business been operating, and could it qualify for credit on its own?
  • How much of the business risk are you willing to tie to your home?
  • How would you keep business and household money separate for records and taxes?

Risks in plain language

  • With a HELOC, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • A slow season or a lost customer does not pause HELOC payments.
  • HELOC payments can change and can jump when the repayment period starts.
  • A personal guarantee on a business line can also make you personally responsible for the debt.
  • How interest is treated for taxes depends on how the money is used. Ask a tax professional.

Common questions

Can I use a HELOC for my business?
Generally yes, within each lender's rules, but the debt is yours personally and your home is the collateral.
What is a personal guarantee?
It is a promise that you will repay the business debt yourself if the business does not. Many lenders ask for one from small business owners.
Where can I learn about business loan programs?
The SBA website explains the business loan programs it works on with lenders, such as 7(a), 504 and microloans, and how to find a participating lender. The SBA does not lend directly for these programs, and we are not connected to the SBA.
