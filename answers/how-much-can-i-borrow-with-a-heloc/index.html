How much can I borrow with a HELOC?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

The lender decides how much you can borrow with a HELOC, based mainly on your home's value, what you already owe on it, your credit history and your income.

Many lenders set the line as a percentage of your home's value minus what you owe on your mortgage, so your credit line is often smaller than your full equity, and each lender sets its own limit.

Questions to ask yourself

  • How much do you actually need for this plan, and could you draw less than the full line?
  • Could you keep up the payments if you drew the whole line and your rate went up?
  • How much equity do you want to leave in the home in case prices fall or you need to sell?

Risks in plain language

  • With a HELOC, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • A large line can make it easy to borrow more than you planned.
  • The lender may freeze or lower your line, for example if your home's value falls, so do not count on the full line always being there.
  • HELOC payments can change and can jump when the repayment period starts.

Common questions

Do I have to borrow the whole credit line?
No. The credit line is the most you can draw. You choose how much to draw during the draw period, and you repay what you draw.
Does a paid off home mean a bigger line?
With no mortgage, more of the home's value counts as equity, which can raise what a lender is willing to offer. Your credit history and income still matter.
Why might a lender offer less than I asked for?
Lenders weigh your home's value, the loans already on it, your credit history and your income. A lower value or more debt can mean a smaller line.
Can the lender lower my line later?
Yes. The CFPB and the FTC both note that a lender may freeze or reduce a line of credit in certain situations, such as a drop in your home's value.
