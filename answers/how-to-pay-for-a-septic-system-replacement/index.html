How do I pay for a septic system replacement?

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 21, 2026.

Start with your local health department and any state, county or USDA septic programs where you live, then compare a home equity loan or line, a personal loan and savings, keeping in mind that a home equity loan or line is secured by your home.

The EPA says repairing or replacing a malfunctioning conventional septic system can cost between $5,000 and $15,000, and alternative systems can cost even more.

The USDA home repair program

  • USDA says the maximum loan is $40,000 and the maximum grant is $10,000, and the two can be combined for up to $50,000.
  • You must own and live in the home, be unable to get affordable credit elsewhere, and have household income within the county low or very low limit.
  • Grants are only for homeowners 62 or older within the very low income limit, and must be repaid if the home is sold in less than three years.
  • USDA's eligibility site shows whether your address is in an eligible rural area. This is a government program, not ours.

Questions to ask yourself

  • Has an inspector or your health department confirmed the system must be replaced, or could a repair do?
  • What permits does your town or county require, and who will design the new system?
  • Is there a state, county or town program where you live, and does it need to approve the work before it starts?
  • Could your budget handle the payment if your rate went up or the repayment period started?
  • Do you plan to sell soon? You are generally required to pay off a HELOC in full when you sell.

Risks in plain language

  • If you use a home equity loan or line, your home is the collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • Payments can change. Many HELOCs have a variable interest rate, so the payment can move even if you borrow nothing new.
  • Payments can jump at repayment. After the draw period ends, payments are often much higher, and some plans require a large balloon payment.
  • The lender can freeze or reduce the line, for example if your home's value falls.
  • Some septic programs will not pay you back for work you already paid for, so ask before you pay a contractor.
  • Some program loans are recorded against your property and come due when you sell. Ask how yours is repaid.

Common questions

How much does it cost to replace a septic system?
The EPA says repairing or replacing a malfunctioning conventional system can cost between $5,000 and $15,000, and alternative systems can cost even more. Your soil, your site and local rules affect the price, so get written estimates.
Are there grants for septic replacement?
Some. The EPA lists federal and state programs, and USDA offers home repair grants to homeowners 62 or older with very low incomes in eligible rural areas. Your county environmental health or public health department can tell you about local programs.
How often should a septic system be inspected?
The EPA says that in general a septic tank should be inspected every 1 to 3 years and pumped every 3 to 5 years. Regular care can help you catch problems before a full replacement is needed.
Is HELOC interest tax deductible for a septic replacement?
IRS Publication 936 says interest on a home equity line is deductible as home mortgage interest only when the money buys, builds or substantially improves the home that secures it, and only if you itemize. Ask a tax professional whether your septic work counts as a substantial improvement in your situation.
What about financing the contractor offers?
The FTC says never to agree to financing through your contractor without shopping around and comparing loan terms, and never to sign a document you have not read or that has blank spaces.
Can I cancel a HELOC after I sign?
On your main home, federal law generally gives you three business days to cancel a home equity line or loan in writing, counted from when you sign and receive the required disclosures and notices.
