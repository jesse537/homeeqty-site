National home equity snapshot, November 2026

Written by Home Equity Lending. Last updated November 18, 2026.

In 2025, 41.0% of U.S. owner occupied homes (35,883,325 of 87,511,734) had no mortgage or other home loan, from 26.5% in the District of Columbia to 56.6% in West Virginia, according to the Census Bureau's American Community Survey.

U.S. homeowners held about $35.8 trillion in home equity at the end of the second quarter of 2026, about 72% of the value of the real estate households own, according to the Federal Reserve.

About 7.5% of owner occupied homes carry a home equity loan, according to the Census Bureau's 2025 American Community Survey.

What the numbers show

  • Most of the value of U.S. homes is equity, not debt. At the end of the second quarter of 2026, debt made up roughly 28% of the value of household real estate.
  • Home equity borrowing is still a small share of homes, about 7.5% in the 2025 survey, ranging from 3.3% in Texas to 13.5% in Rhode Island.
  • Balances on home equity lines of credit are growing. The New York Fed reports they rose $23 billion in the third quarter of 2026, the largest quarterly rise since 2005, to $481 billion.
  • Home prices rose over the year in most states. FHFA's index rose the most in Alaska (8.3%) and fell in four states: New Mexico, Washington, Colorado and California.

A HELOC or home equity loan uses your home as collateral. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.

The figures on this page describe homeowners as a group. They do not tell you how much you could borrow, and they are not an offer of credit.

How we built this

  • National equity figures are the Federal Reserve's own numbers from its Financial Accounts of the United States (Z.1), as published on FRED. They cover real estate owned by households as a whole. The Federal Reserve's next release is scheduled for December 10, 2026.
  • Debt balances are as reported in the New York Fed's Quarterly Report on Household Debt and Credit for the third quarter of 2026, released November 10, 2026.
  • State shares are our own calculations from Census Bureau table B25081 (mortgage status) in the 2025 American Community Survey 1 year estimates. Owned free and clear is homes without a mortgage divided by all owner occupied homes. With a home equity loan adds the survey's three categories that include one (home equity loan only, both a second mortgage and a home equity loan, and a home equity loan without a primary mortgage) and divides by all owner occupied homes. Every share is computed from the exact published counts and rounded only at the end. For example, 6,538,749 of 87,511,734 owner occupied homes is 7.47%, shown as 7.5%.
  • The survey uses the term home equity loan and does not ask about lines of credit as a separate category, so we report the survey's own term.
  • Survey figures are estimates from a sample and have margins of error. The CSV gives the Census Bureau's 90% margins of error for counts and home values, and our approximations for the shares using the Census Bureau's published formula for proportions.
  • Median home value is the survey estimate from table B25077, based on what owners report their homes are worth.
  • Home price change is FHFA's seasonally adjusted purchase only House Price Index for the second quarter of 2026, as published in FHFA's state summary table. The index is built from sales of single family homes with mortgages backed by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac.
  • We do not estimate home equity dollars by state, because we did not find a free public source that reports both home values and mortgage debt by state on the same basis. Puerto Rico is not included.

Risks in plain language

  • A HELOC or home equity loan is secured by your home. If you cannot repay, you could lose your home.
  • Borrowing against equity turns part of that equity back into debt.
  • Home values can fall, as they did over the past year in four states. If your home loses value, a lender may freeze or lower a home equity line.
  • These are totals, averages and survey estimates. Your own equity depends on what your home is worth and what you owe on it.

Common questions

How much home equity do U.S. homeowners have?
About $35.8 trillion at the end of the second quarter of 2026, according to the Federal Reserve's Financial Accounts of the United States. That equals about 72% of the value of the real estate households own. It is a total for all households, not the equity in any one home.
How many homeowners have a home equity loan or HELOC?
The Census Bureau's 2025 American Community Survey estimates that 6,538,749 of 87,511,734 owner occupied homes, or 7.5%, have a home equity loan. The survey does not ask about lines of credit as a separate category. Separately, the New York Fed reports that Americans owed $481 billion on home equity lines of credit in the third quarter of 2026.
How many homes are owned free and clear?
35,883,325 of 87,511,734 owner occupied homes, or 41.0%, had no mortgage or other home loan in 2025, according to the American Community Survey. The share ranges from 26.5% in the District of Columbia to 56.6% in West Virginia.
Which states have the most home equity?
We do not estimate home equity dollars by state, because we did not find a free public source that reports both home values and mortgage debt by state on the same basis. On the measures in our table, West Virginia has the largest share of owner occupied homes owned free and clear, 56.6%, followed by Mississippi (52.7%) and New Mexico (50.0%), according to the Census Bureau's 2025 American Community Survey. Hawaii ($901,500), California ($770,700) and the District of Columbia ($715,500) have the highest median home values. A high home value does not by itself mean high equity, because it does not account for what owners owe.
Does home equity mean I can borrow that amount?
No. Equity is the value of a home minus what is owed on it. A lender decides how much, if anything, a homeowner can borrow, and any home equity loan or line uses the home as collateral.
